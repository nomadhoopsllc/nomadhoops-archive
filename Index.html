Streetball Crews
Founded 1993 — Streetball's biggest stage
01
The Mixtape TourEst. late 1990s
AND1 built its identity on street basketball, releasing a series of mixtape videos capturing playground legends — Hot Sauce, Skip 2 My Lou, The Professor, Escalade, and others — performing in front of packed crowds. The mixtapes became a cultural phenomenon, eventually leading to the live AND1 Mixtape Tour, which traveled to cities across the country pairing AND1 players against local street legends.
02
Cultural ImpactStreetball goes mainstream
AND1's tapes were many fans' first exposure to streetball style — crossovers, trick handles, and showmanship prioritized over traditional fundamentals. The tour and tapes helped launch or elevate careers for numerous players and created a template later carried forward by digital-era platforms like Ballislife and The Next Chapter.
Vancouver, BC — Underground scene
01
OriginsVancouver underground
The Notic represents the Vancouver streetball scene — grassroots events built around local talent, DIY production, and a tight-knit community rather than national media attention. Nomadhoops has collaborated directly on Notic events, including annual gatherings at Grays Park.
02
The Notic DayAnnual event
An annual community event bringing Vancouver's streetball scene together for competition, content, and culture — Nomadhoops has been an active collaborator in recent years, including content production alongside King Handles.
Est. 2010 — Founder: Lorenzo "Zobeone Kenobi" Baxter
01
Origins2010
Founded by Lorenzo "Zobeone Kenobi" Baxter as a platform dedicated to preserving, representing, and advancing streetball culture. The name reflects a life built around going wherever basketball is being played — Nomadhoops has never been tied to one court, one city, or one league. Its identity is rooted in movement, competition, history, culture, and connection, captured in the brand's defining statement: "We have no homecourt, but we'll take yours."
02
The FounderZobeone Kenobi
Lorenzo Baxter brings firsthand experience as a player, historian, content creator, archivist, and cultural connector. His background includes recognition and participation connected to the AND1 Mixtape Tour era, the Miller Lite Go Pro Challenge on NBA TV, SLAM Magazine, and USA Basketball's 3x3 National Player Pool Alumni under the FIBA 3x3 format. That experience lets Nomadhoops document the culture from the perspective of someone who has lived it — not simply observed it.
03
The MissionDocument & Preserve
Streetball history has often been scattered across VHS tapes, DVDs, magazines, old websites, photographs, and personal memories — many influential players, events, and courts were never properly recorded. Nomadhoops works to bring those stories together in one place, before they're lost.
04
Four ResponsibilitiesCompete · Connect · Document · Preserve
Nomadhoops operates on four pillars: competing at the highest level the culture allows, connecting streetball communities across cities and generations, documenting the people and moments that define the game, and preserving that record for those who come after.
Legendary Parks

Rucker Park

Harlem, New York. The most storied court in streetball history — home to the Rucker Tournament since the 1950s, where NBA legends and playground legends have shared the same blacktop. A cornerstone reference point for streetball culture worldwide.
